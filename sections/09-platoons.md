# Platoons, Commitment, and the Shop

## What is a platoon?

Platoons are our on-brand way of organizing the team by discipline. Think of them as sub-teams or departments. Students indicated their platoon preference in the application form, but are not locked into one specific platoon for the entirety of the season. Consult the lead mentor of the platoon you wish to work with before collaborating with the group.

| Platoon | Description |
|---|---|
| Design | Designs robots using CAD software |
| Manufacturing | Creates custom metal and plastic parts for robots |
| Build | Assembles robot parts according to designs |
| Control Systems | Wires and programs robot motors and sensors |
| Outreach | Plans and conducts community events; designs and presents for the impact award |
| Media and Marketing | Documents and promotes the team |

## How often do I need to attend?

The answer to this question greatly depends on what you expect to get out of the program. We regularly tell our students, "You get out of robotics what you put into it". This essentially means that you get to decide how involved you are with the team.

Our competition season weekday meetings are only scheduled to 6:00 pm, but oftentimes groups of students will stick around an extra hour to work on their projects in order to meet deadlines. The reality is that progress will continue with or without you. It is up to you to decide if you want to be a casual member or fully dive into the robotics lifestyle, or anything in between.

While students occasionally choose to push themselves to work some extra hours at times when deadlines are approaching, for the most part working long nights for extended periods indicates a fundamental failure in planning or communication. If this happens at 7028, it's a sign that something needs to be reevaluated and corrected. We work hard to attract the most capable and intelligent students, and we want them to stick around and have a good balance between robotics and the rest of the important stuff in life. If you find yourself putting in long hours, or just generally feel like that balance is out of whack, be sure to raise the issue with your lead mentor or a coach.

\begin{tcolorbox}[callout]
We all love robotics. We love being in the shop with our friends. We simply love solving problems together. Being so passionate about anything in life is a huge win, and it is so fulfilling to see our students engaged and excited about STEM. But we seriously need to be careful about over-committing. Robotics should never be prioritized over your mental health or your school work. Coaches and mentors will never hold it against you for taking care of yourself over coming to robotics. Yes, we love what we do, but everyone needs breaks. Always keep in mind that this program is about YOU, not the work.
\end{tcolorbox}

## What do I need to know about the shop?

Our work area consists of five separate rooms (six including the Studio) that are designed for a variety of purposes. There are some amazing benefits to having such a large shop area but also some things to be aware of. Safety glasses are required for all shop areas except the CAD lab and the Studio. For safety reasons, never work alone in any of the shops. Never use equipment that you are not trained on or are unsure about using.

We share these spaces with hundreds of other STMA High School students who use them every day in their technology education classes. The shops do not belong to us; we are guests in the space. Because of this fact, it is important for all members to follow our "Leave no Trace" philosophy, which essentially means clean up after yourself and leave the space the same as you found it.

Although they don't necessarily belong to us, during robotics hours these shops are reserved for us and only us. No one outside of our team is allowed to be in the shops with us during these times. This is for the safety and security of the team.
