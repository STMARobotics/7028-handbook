# Your First Year

## Welcome to 7028!

So you've gone through the application process, registered for the team, and you're finally here at 7028. Congratulations and welcome!

Starting your journey on the team can be overwhelming with the scale of a First Robotics Competition team like 7028. This handbook is designed to ease your worries by providing a clear description of who we are and what you should expect. We want to make this the greatest collaborative STEM experience of your life, and that starts with a positive and productive first year.

A growth mindset is the key to becoming the next 7028 success story. Our greatest members are not necessarily the ones who bring the most skill or ability, but rather they bring a positive mindset and the will to learn and collaborate.

## What should I work on?

Our students are free to pursue any area of the team they are passionate about, even if that area doesn't exist yet! There is no expectation that new members dive in and become super involved their first year. In fact, we recommend that every new student start with a goal of absorbing and learning as much as possible their first year. Be present and be ready to assist, but be careful about setting your sights too high as a rookie. Your 7028 career is years in the making. Take your time and enjoy every minute.

Part of being successful on 7028 is your ability to seek out and find new projects on your own. Checking in with mentors to receive tasks is a great way to start, but they may not always have something specific for you to do. This is where becoming a self-starter and seeking new tasks and new ways to add value independently is crucial.

## What if I don't have the skills/ability?

Our coaches and mentors often say things like, "The build season is not the time to learn new skills." But if this is true, how can we expect our students to learn new things? Isn't that the entire point of the program? Yes, it is true that the build season does not include any formal education. Every day is a race to complete the next set of goals before the competition, leaving little room for formal education and training. Expecting students to have the skills to be successful while not actually teaching them anything is not the intention, and this is why we have our First Tech Challenge teams.

If you're looking to sharpen your robotics skills before the FRC season begins, joining a FIRST Tech Challenge (FTC) team provides the most effective experience. FTC allows students to develop a wide range of robotics skills on a smaller scale within a real competitive season. In the past, our team struggled to make preseason training both engaging and valuable, but we've realized that the best preparation for our students is for them to keep competing.

## Can I be included the next time 7028 is deciding X?

Yes! There's no secret decision-making platoon (*well, maybe except for the Fortnite platoon*). No matter what project, you're already invited. All you have to do is either (1) Start working on it, or (2) Start talking to all the people who you think might be working on it already and find out how to best be valuable. You will be welcomed — there is no approval process or red tape involved. Quite the opposite — it's your job to insert yourself wherever you think you should be.
